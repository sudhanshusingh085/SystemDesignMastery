High-Level Design (HLD) Overview

Welcome to High-Level Design! High-level design (HLD) is the macro view of your system. It's about how different components like servers, databases, caches, and message queues connect to solve large-scale problems.

What is High-Level Design?

Imagine you are building a city. Before you decide where to put the power outlets in a specific house (which is Low-Level Design), you need to figure out where the residential zones, commercial zones, power plants, and highways will be. This macro-level planning is High-Level Design (HLD).

In software, HLD involves selecting the right broad architecture and technologies to ensure the system is scalable, reliable, and performant. You focus on the boxes and arrows that represent the flow of data.

📖 System Architecture: The conceptual model that defines the structure, behavior, and more views of a system. It's the blueprint.

When is HLD used in interviews vs real-world?

In Interviews

  • Focuses on identifying bottlenecks and trade-offs.
  • Usually done on a whiteboard (or virtual whiteboard).
  • Expected to cover breadth first, then go deep into 1-2 components.
  • Time-bound (typically 45-60 minutes).

In the Real World

  • Involves writing detailed design documents (RFCs, Tech Specs).
  • Requires extensive debate and prototyping.
  • Cost and specific team expertise play huge roles in tech choices.
  • Evolves over time as business requirements change.
